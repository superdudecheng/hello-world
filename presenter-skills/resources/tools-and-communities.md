# 🛠️ Tools & Communities

Speaking improves fastest with **real audiences and honest feedback**. Solo
practice has a ceiling; a room full of people does not.

## Communities — practice in front of people

- **Toastmasters International.** The gold standard. Local clubs meet weekly,
  give you speaking slots, and provide structured feedback and evaluations. Low
  cost, worldwide, welcoming to beginners. → https://www.toastmasters.org
- **Local meetups.** Search Meetup.com for "public speaking," "storytelling," or
  "improv" groups in your area.
- **Improv classes.** Improv is a shortcut to *presence*, spontaneity, and
  killing the fear of the unexpected. Highly recommended alongside this program.
- **The Moth StorySLAMs / open mics.** Put your name in the hat. Five minutes,
  no notes, real stakes. Nothing builds you faster. → https://themoth.org
- **Speaking clubs at work / school.** Or start one. Even 4 people meeting to
  give each other 3-minute talks works.

## Feedback tools

- **Your phone camera.** Non-negotiable. Record every practice run. Watch it
  back with the sound off (body language), then with sound only (voice).
- **AI speech-feedback apps** (e.g. **Yoodli**, **Orai**, **Poised**). They
  analyze pace, filler words ("um," "like"), and pauses. Useful for solo drilling
  — but no substitute for a human audience.
- **A trusted feedback partner.** Give them the [rubric](../practice/self-assessment-rubric.md)
  so their notes are specific ("you rushed the ending") not vague ("it was good").

## Preparation & delivery tools

- **Slides:** Keep it simple. Google Slides, Keynote, or PowerPoint are all fine.
  Read [the slides module](../curriculum/05-visuals-and-slides.md) *before* you
  open any of them — most people build slides far too early.
- **Teleprompter apps** for rehearsing scripted talks (video intros, keynotes).
- **A visible timer** or a smartwatch. Rehearse to time, every time.
- **Notes:** Learn to speak from a handful of keywords on an index card, not a
  full script. See [content & structure](../curriculum/01-content-and-structure.md).

## Where to give your first talks (in order of difficulty)

1. To your phone camera (private)
2. To one supportive friend
3. To a small, friendly group (Toastmasters, a meetup)
4. A low-stakes work setting (team demo, stand-up update)
5. A lightning talk at a meetup (5 min)
6. A full talk / conference session / keynote

Climb this ladder deliberately. Each rung makes the next one feel normal.
