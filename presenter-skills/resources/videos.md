# 🎬 Video Resources

The heart of this project. Watching great speakers — and watching people *teach*
speaking — is the fastest way to build your instinct for what works.

**How to watch (this matters):** Don't watch passively. For every talk, keep the
[analysis worksheet](#watch-actively-the-4-question-worksheet) at the bottom of
this page open and answer the four questions. You're not watching to be
entertained; you're reverse-engineering a craft.

---

## 1. Learn the craft — teachers who explain *how* it's done

These teach the mechanics directly. Start here.

- **"How to speak so that people want to listen" — Julian Treasure (TED)**
  The single best 10-minute primer on voice: the 4 vocal foundations (register,
  timbre, prosody, pace), the 7 deadly sins of speaking, and warm-up exercises.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/julian_treasure_how_to_speak_so_that_people_want_to_listen

- **"The secret structure of great talks" — Nancy Duarte (TEDxEast)**
  Reveals the "sparkline" — the pattern behind history's most moving speeches
  (including MLK's "I Have a Dream"). Essential for structuring content.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/nancy_duarte_the_secret_structure_of_great_talks

- **"Talk Like TED" author Carmine Gallo — talks & interviews (YouTube search)**
  Distills what the most-viewed TED talks have in common: emotion, novelty, and
  memorability. Search: `Carmine Gallo Talk Like TED`.

- **"Your body language may shape who you are" — Amy Cuddy (TED)**
  On presence and how posture affects confidence. (Note: the specific "power
  pose → hormone" claim is scientifically contested — watch it for the ideas
  about presence and self-talk, not as settled science.)
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/amy_cuddy_your_body_language_may_shape_who_you_are

- **Toastmasters International — official YouTube channel**
  Free tutorials on gestures, vocal variety, evaluations, and full World
  Championship of Public Speaking performances to study.
  → https://www.youtube.com/@Toastmasters

- **Stanford GSB — "Think Fast, Talk Smart" (Matt Abrahams)**
  A masterclass on *spontaneous* speaking — handling Q&A, toasts, and being put
  on the spot. Consistently one of the most-watched business-school talks.
  → Search: `Think Fast Talk Smart Matt Abrahams Stanford`

- **"The Art of Stillness / speaking without notes" — practical channels**
  Channels worth subscribing to: **Vinh Giang** (voice, accents, storytelling),
  **Conor Neill** (rhetoric and persuasion), **Charisma on Command** (analysis
  of what makes speakers magnetic), **Firm Learning** and **Nathaniel Drew**
  (structure and clarity).

## 2. Study the masters — talks to watch *as an audience member*, then dissect

The greatest examples of the craft. Watch once for the experience, then again
with the worksheet.

**Modern talks (great structure & delivery):**
- **Sir Ken Robinson — "Do schools kill creativity?"** The most-viewed TED talk
  ever. A masterclass in humor, timing, and warmth.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/sir_ken_robinson_do_schools_kill_creativity
- **Brené Brown — "The power of vulnerability."** Storytelling and emotional
  honesty at its best.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/brene_brown_the_power_of_vulnerability
- **Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie — "The danger of a single story."** Narrative
  structure and moral clarity.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/chimamanda_ngozi_adichie_the_danger_of_a_single_story
- **Hans Rosling — "The best stats you've ever seen."** How to make data
  thrilling. A model for technical presenters.
  → https://www.ted.com/talks/hans_rosling_the_best_stats_you_ve_ever_seen

**Historic oratory (rhetoric & command):**
- **Martin Luther King Jr. — "I Have a Dream."** Study the repetition
  (anaphora), rhythm, and build. Search: `MLK I Have a Dream full speech`.
- **Steve Jobs — Stanford Commencement Address (2005).** Three stories, one
  theme. A near-perfect example of the rule of three and personal narrative.
  → https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UF8uR6Z6KLc
- **Winston Churchill speeches / Barack Obama's 2004 DNC keynote.** Study
  cadence, pause, and how great orators use silence. Search these titles.

## 3. Free full courses & lectures

- **University of Washington — "Introduction to Public Speaking" (Dr. Matt
  McGarrity)** on Coursera. A genuinely excellent free university course. Audit
  it for $0. Search: `Coursera Introduction to Public Speaking McGarrity`.
- **Harvard / edX — "Rhetoric: The Art of Persuasive Writing and Public
  Speaking."** Search: `edX Rhetoric persuasive public speaking`.
- **TED-Ed lessons on public speaking & rhetoric** — short, animated, excellent.
  → https://www.youtube.com/@TEDEd
- **"How to sound smart in your TEDx Talk" — Will Stephen (TEDxNewYork).** A
  brilliant, funny 6 minutes on how delivery signals confidence *independent of
  content*. Watch it — you'll never forget it.
  → Search: `Will Stephen How to sound smart in your TEDx Talk`

## 4. Genre-specific

- **Pitching / business:** Search `best startup pitch TechCrunch Disrupt` and
  study Y Combinator's demo-day guidance on YouTube.
- **Technical talks:** Watch strong conference speakers (e.g. talks from
  **Strange Loop**, **PyCon keynotes**). Study how they explain hard ideas.
- **Storytelling:** **The Moth** (themoth.org / YouTube) — pure narrative craft,
  no slides, just a person and a microphone.

## Watch actively: the 4-question worksheet

For **every** video you watch in this program, answer these. Keep a running log
(you can use [`../practice/progress-tracker.md`](../practice/progress-tracker.md)).

1. **Hook:** How did they open? What did they do in the first 30 seconds to earn
   my attention?
2. **Structure:** What was the single core message? Could I draw the shape of the
   talk (its beginning, turns, and end) on a napkin?
3. **Delivery:** Note *one specific thing* about their voice, pause, gesture, or
   movement that worked — and one thing I could steal.
4. **Emotion:** Where did I feel something? Why there? What technique created it?

> A great habit: watch one talk this way per week. In a year you'll have
> reverse-engineered 50 of the best speakers alive.
