# Module 7 — Nerves & Performance Mindset

> *"I get nervous before every talk. The day I stop, I'll know I've stopped
> caring."* — many great speakers, in various words

Nerves are not a sign you're bad at this. They're a sign you care. Even veteran
performers feel them. The goal is **not to eliminate nerves** — that's impossible
and undesirable — but to **channel the energy** so it fuels you instead of
sabotaging you. This module is your pre-talk toolkit.

**Watch:** Amy Cuddy on presence (with the caveat noted in
[videos](../resources/videos.md)) — useful for the mindset, not the biochemistry.

---

## Reframe #1: Fear and excitement are the same thing

Physiologically, anxiety and excitement are **nearly identical**: racing heart,
adrenaline, heightened energy, alertness. The *only* difference is the label your
brain puts on it.

Trying to "calm down" fights your body's arousal and usually fails. Instead,
**reframe** it: tell yourself *"I'm excited."* Research (Alison Wood Brooks,
Harvard) found that people who said "I am excited" out loud before a stressful
performance did measurably better than those who tried to calm down. Same energy,
better story.

> Before you go on, say — out loud if you can — **"I'm excited."** Mean it.

## Reframe #2: It's about them, not you

Nerves feed on **self-focus**: *"What will they think of me? Will I mess up? Do I
look nervous?"* All spotlight on you.

Shift the spotlight to the audience: *"What do they need? How can I help them?
What do I want them to walk away with?"* When you're genuinely focused on serving
the audience, there's simply less mental room left for self-conscious fear. This
single reframe — service over performance — is the most powerful one in this whole
project (see [Module 0](00-foundations.md)).

## Reframe #3: They can't see your nerves

What feels like a raging internal storm is nearly invisible from the outside. Your
pounding heart, churning stomach, and racing thoughts are **private**. Audiences
routinely rate speakers as "calm and confident" who felt they were falling apart.
Knowing this breaks the doom loop of *being nervous about looking nervous.*

## Reframe #4: The audience is on your side

You imagine a jury waiting for you to fail. The reality: the audience **wants you
to succeed.** They gave you their time; they want it to be worth it. They're
rooting for you, not against you. Nobody in that room is hoping to watch you
flounder. Walk out assuming goodwill — because it's there.

## The pre-talk physical routine

Nerves are physical, so part of the fix is physical. Build a routine and run it
every time:

1. **Breathe — slow and low.** The fastest nervous-system reset. Try **box
   breathing**: inhale 4 counts, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Repeat 4–5 times.
   Long exhales are the key — they switch on the "calm" branch of your nervous
   system.
2. **Warm up your voice and body** (see [drills](../practice/drills.md)). This
   discharges nervous energy *and* prepares your instrument. Shake out your hands,
   roll your shoulders, loosen your jaw.
3. **Move.** A brisk walk or a few discreet stretches burns off excess adrenaline
   before it comes out as shaking.
4. **Power posture.** Stand tall, open, and grounded for a minute beforehand.
   Regardless of the contested hormone science, an upright, open posture reliably
   makes *you feel* more ready and breathe better.
5. **Hydrate.** A dry mouth is a nerves classic. Sip water. Avoid too much caffeine
   — it amplifies the jitters.

## The mental routine

- **Visualize success.** Athletes do this: vividly imagine yourself delivering
  well, the audience engaged, the strong finish. Rehearse the *feeling* of it
  going right. Your brain doesn't fully distinguish vivid rehearsal from reality.
- **Positive self-talk.** Swap "I'm going to blank" for "I know this. I've
  prepared. I've got something worth sharing." Talk to yourself like a good coach,
  not a critic.
- **Lower the stakes.** This one talk is not a referendum on your worth. It's one
  talk. Even if it goes badly, life continues and you'll learn from it. Perfection
  is not the bar — connection is.
- **Accept imperfection in advance.** You *will* stumble on a word or lose your
  place at some point. That's normal and the audience barely notices. Decide now
  that a small slip is fine, so it can't rattle you.

---

## Preparation is the ultimate anti-anxiety drug

The deepest source of confidence isn't a trick — it's **knowing you're prepared.**
Nothing calms nerves like having rehearsed your talk out loud, on your feet,
multiple times.

- **Rehearse for real.** Not in your head — out loud, standing, ideally to a
  person or camera. Aim for enough reps that the *flow* is automatic (so a nervous
  brain can coast on muscle memory).
- **Lock your opening cold.** Most nerves peak in the first 30–60 seconds. If your
  first lines are on autopilot, you'll settle fast. Memorize the opening (and
  closing) word-for-word even though the middle is from keywords.
- **Know your material, not a script.** Deep familiarity with your *ideas* means
  that even if you lose your exact words, you can't lose your way.

## In-the-moment rescues

Things go wrong. Here's your kit:
- **Mind goes blank?** Pause. Breathe. Glance at your keyword card. Or simply
  restate your last point — it buys time and often unlocks the next one. The
  silence feels long to you and normal to them.
- **Shaky voice / hands?** Slow down, plant your feet, and take one deliberate
  breath. Grounding physically steadies the voice. Gesturing purposefully also
  hides a hand tremor.
- **You flub a line?** Don't apologize or flag it — most people didn't notice.
  Just carry on. Drawing attention to a mistake is what makes it a mistake.
- **Tech fails?** Smile, acknowledge it lightly, and keep going with your no-slides
  backup (see [Module 5](05-visuals-and-slides.md)). Grace under a glitch actually
  *builds* credibility.

---

## 🎯 This module's exercises

1. **Build your routine:** Write your personal pre-talk checklist (breathing +
   warm-up + one mental reframe). Put it on a card. Run it before every practice
   session so it's automatic on the real day.
2. **Practice box breathing** daily this week until it's second nature.
3. **The reframe rep:** Before your next recorded practice, say *"I'm excited"* out
   loud and focus on one thing the audience will gain. Notice the difference.
4. **Over-rehearse the opening:** Memorize your talk's first 30 seconds until you
   could say it half-asleep.
