# Module 4 — Storytelling

> *"Stories are just data with a soul."* — Brené Brown

If you master only one skill from this entire project, make it this one.
Storytelling is the highest-leverage tool a speaker has. Facts inform, but
**stories move** — they're how humans have transmitted meaning for 50,000 years,
and the brain is wired to receive them. A statistic is forgotten by lunch; a good
story is retold for years.

**Watch:** Brené Brown, *"The power of vulnerability"*; and one story from **The
Moth** (see [videos](../resources/videos.md)) — pure narrative, no slides.

---

## Why stories work

- **Attention:** "Once, when I was 19…" makes people lean in automatically. The
  brain can't help but wonder what happens next.
- **Memory:** We remember narratives far better than lists. A story is a delivery
  vehicle that smuggles your point past the audience's defenses and into
  long-term memory.
- **Emotion & trust:** Stories — especially vulnerable, personal ones — build
  connection. Neurologically, a good story syncs the audience's brains with the
  speaker's. They *feel* what you felt.
- **Persuasion:** People argue with your claims but not with your experiences. A
  story lets them draw the conclusion themselves, which they'll believe far more
  than one you assert.

---

## The anatomy of a story

Every story needs these bones:

1. **A relatable character** (often *you*). We need someone to care about.
2. **A goal or desire** — what the character wants.
3. **An obstacle / conflict / stakes** — this is the engine. **No conflict, no
   story.** Something must go wrong, be at risk, or be hard.
4. **A turning point** — the moment of change, decision, or realization.
5. **A resolution** — how it ends, and what changed.
6. **A point** — why you told it. The bridge from the story to your core message.

If your "story" has no conflict, it's an anecdote, and it will fall flat.
Something must be *at stake*.

---

## Two structures to steal

### The Story Spine (Pixar)
> Once upon a time, there was ___.
> Every day, ___.
> Until one day, ___.  ← *the disruption*
> Because of that, ___.
> Because of that, ___.  ← *escalating consequences*
> Until finally, ___.  ← *the climax*
> And ever since then, ___.  ← *the new normal / the lesson*

### The Hero's Journey (compressed)
Ordinary world → a call/challenge → struggle & setbacks → a low point →
breakthrough → transformed return. You don't need the full mythic version — just
**ordinary → disruption → struggle → change.**

## Craft techniques that make stories land

- **Show, don't tell.** Not "I was nervous" but "my hands were shaking so hard I
  dropped my notes." Let the audience *conclude* the emotion from the detail.
- **Sensory detail.** One or two vivid, specific details ("the smell of burnt
  coffee," "the fluorescent hum of the empty office") make a scene real. Specific
  beats general, every time.
- **Start in the action.** Skip the throat-clearing setup. Open *in* the moment:
  "The phone rang at 3 a.m." Then fill in what's needed.
- **Use dialogue.** "She looked at me and said, 'You're not ready.'" Dialogue is
  more vivid and immediate than narration.
- **Control the pace.** Slow down and add detail at the emotional peak; speed
  through the parts that just connect the dots.
- **Make the stakes clear.** We need to know *why it matters* what happens.

## Finding your stories

You have hundreds of usable stories — you just haven't catalogued them. Prompts:
- A time you failed, and what it taught you.
- A moment something changed your mind.
- The hardest decision you've made.
- A small, ordinary moment that turned out to matter.
- A time you were surprised, embarrassed, afraid, or proud.

The best speaking stories are often **small and personal**, not epic. A
five-minute story about a single ordinary moment, told well, beats a grand tale
told vaguely. And **vulnerability** — a real failure or fear — connects far more
than a highlight reel. (See Matthew Dix's *Storyworthy* in
[books](../resources/books.md) for the best method on this.)

Keep a running **story bank**: whenever a moment from your life could illustrate
a point, jot it down. Over time you'll have a story for any occasion.

---

## Connecting the story to your point

A story without a point is just a detour. Always build the bridge:
- Tell the story, land its emotional beat, **then** make the turn: *"I tell you
  this because…"* or simply let the lesson emerge.
- One story should serve **one point.** Don't overload it.
- The point should feel *earned* by the story, not bolted on.

---

## 🎯 This module's exercises

1. **Build your story bank:** Write down **five true stories** from your life —
   just a title and one line each. Keep adding over time.
2. **Tell one in 90 seconds:** Pick one story. Tell it out loud, no notes, in
   90 seconds. Make sure it has a clear conflict and a turning point. Record it.
3. **Add craft:** Retell it applying **"show, don't tell"** and **one vivid
   sensory detail.** Record again and compare — the second version will feel
   noticeably more alive.
4. **Build the bridge:** Attach your story to a point you might make in a real
   talk. Practice the transition from story to lesson.
