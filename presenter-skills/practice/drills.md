# 🏋️ Daily Drills

Ten minutes a day beats a three-hour cram. These drills build the underlying
muscles of speaking so that when you're on stage, the fundamentals run on
autopilot. Pick a few each day.

> **The golden rule:** almost every drill should be done **out loud, standing up,
> and recorded** at least sometimes. Speaking is physical. Reading these silently
> does nothing.

---

## 🔥 Vocal warm-up (do before every practice & every real talk — ~3 min)

Never speak cold. Run this in the car, shower, or a spare room:

1. **Breathing (30s):** Hand on belly. Inhale low and slow (belly out), exhale
   longer than you inhale. 5 rounds. Sets up breath support.
2. **Lip trills / "brrr" (30s):** Blow air through loose lips like a motorboat,
   sliding pitch up and down. Relaxes the lips and voice.
3. **Humming & sirens (30s):** Hum, then "ng" glide from your lowest to highest
   pitch and back. Warms your range and resonance.
4. **Tongue twisters (60s):** Over-articulate, slow then fast:
   - "Red leather, yellow leather" (×5)
   - "Unique New York" (×5)
   - "The lips, the teeth, the tip of the tongue"
   - "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers"
5. **Jaw & face (30s):** Big exaggerated chewing, yawns, and face scrunches to
   release tension.

## 🎙️ Delivery drills

### The pause drill
Give a 1-minute talk. Force a **full 2-second silence** before and after your key
sentence. Record it. Watch how commanding the silence looks (it always feels
longer than it is).

### The filler-word killer
Record 2 minutes of unscripted talk on any topic. Count your "um / uh / like / so
/ you know." Re-record, replacing every filler with a **closed-mouth pause.**
Repeat weekly and watch the count drop.

### The vocal-range stretch
Read a children's book aloud with *wildly* exaggerated character voices and pitch
swings. This expands the range you'll draw on. Then give a normal talk keeping
~25% of that expressiveness.

### The pace-shift drill
Take one paragraph. Deliver it three ways: too fast, too slow, and *varied*
(fast on energy, slow on the key line). Feel the difference varied pacing makes.

### The whisper-to-projection drill
Say the same sentence at 5 volumes, from near-whisper to back-row projection —
all from breath support, never throat strain. Builds volume control.

## 🧠 Content & thinking-on-your-feet drills

### Impromptu 60 (the best all-around drill)
Pick a **random topic** (open a book to a random word, or use a prompt list).
Speak on it for **60 seconds** using a structure:
- **PREP:** Point → Reason → Example → Point
- or **Past → Present → Future**
- or **What? → So what? → Now what?**
Do one every day. This is the single most effective drill for becoming quick,
composed, and articulate on the spot.

### The one-sentence distiller
Take any article, meeting, or idea and force yourself to summarize its core in
**one sentence.** Trains the "find the ONE thing" muscle from
[Module 1](../curriculum/01-content-and-structure.md).

### The story-a-day
Each day, recall one small moment from your life and tell it as a 60-second story
with a clear beginning, turn, and point. Builds your
[story bank](../curriculum/04-storytelling.md) and your narrative reflexes.

### The re-explain drill
Explain a complex idea you know well as if to (a) a 5-year-old, (b) a smart
teenager, (c) an expert. Trains you to adapt to your audience.

## 🧍 Body & presence drills

### The mute review
Record any practice talk and watch it **with the sound off.** Spot one nervous
tell (swaying, hidden hands, no eye contact). Fix that one thing next round. (See
the checklist in [Module 3](../curriculum/03-body-language.md).)

### Home-base practice
Practice standing grounded (feet shoulder-width, weight even), gesturing on
purpose, then **returning your hands to a neutral home base.** Do it until "still
and grounded" feels natural, not stiff.

### Eye-contact rounds
Place 3–5 objects around the room. Give a talk, landing a **complete thought** on
one "person" before moving to the next. No sweeping or scanning.

## 🧘 Nerves drills

### Box breathing
Inhale 4 · hold 4 · exhale 4 · hold 4. Five rounds. Practice daily so it's
automatic when you need it before a real talk. (See
[Module 7](../curriculum/07-nerves-and-mindset.md).)

### The "I'm excited" reframe
Before any recorded practice, say **"I'm excited"** out loud and name one thing
the audience will gain. Train the reframe so it's your default on the big day.

---

## 📅 A sample 10-minute daily routine

1. Vocal warm-up (3 min)
2. Impromptu 60 on a random topic (2 min)
3. Story-a-day: one 60-second story (2 min)
4. Watch it back once (2 min) — note **one** thing to improve
5. Box breathing to close (1 min)

Do this most days for 12 weeks and you will be a visibly different speaker.
